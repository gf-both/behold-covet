# Behold / Covet
### How the Torah guards the gaze that would turn the given into the taken
**Book outline — v1**

---

## Front matter

**A note on how this book was made.** The discovery story in miniature; the human–AI collaboration stated plainly (the insights and thesis are the author's; the corpus verification, counter-search, and post-hoc identification of scholarly convergences were machine-assisted); the promise to the reader: every Hebrew claim verified against the Westminster Leningrad Codex, misses reported alongside hits.

**How to read the evidence.** The book's coherence device, stated once and used everywhere: every claim carries one of four tiers. **T1 — On the surface:** the Hebrew word is in the verse (verifiable by anyone). **T2 — Pattern:** a corpus-wide regularity, counted and, where possible, tested against a null. **T3 — Reading (derash):** constructive interpretation, argued but not provable. **T4 — Poetry of the letters:** gematria and letter-structure, offered as the tradition's own art form, never as proof. The gaze-tier badges of the companion site become the book's epistemic ladder.

**A note on Hebrew.** Transliteration guide; no Hebrew required to read the book.

## Part I — The discovery
*The gaze, and how it turns.*

**1. Two verses, one inversion.**
The personal opening: how the author noticed that Genesis 3:6 re-uses the words of 2:9 — inverted, with one substitution (ta'avah for nechmad's innocence). Leitwort, repetition-with-variation, Seidel's law. The claim is planted: the tree didn't change; the gaze did.
*Source material: essay opening; curated tab; readings cards 1–3.*

**2. There is no forbidden tree.**
The moving center (2:9 vs 3:3); the serpent's inverted quotation of the permission; half-truths as the most cowardly lies; desire as the offspring of an invented lack. The garden as the human being; the center as the point of view.
*Source: readings tab; essay §1.*

**3. Cain — the first taking after the gate.** ★ NEW
The missing station, written for the book: Cain's name IS "I have acquired" (kaniti); God's gaze as the first contested resource (sha'ah — He gazed at Abel's offering, not Cain's); the fallen face as the dropped gaze; God's remedy in the vocabulary of the whole system — se'et, "lifting" (4:7); teshukah, the desire-word of Eden, crouching at the door. The first murder is a covet-gaze aimed at the Giver's eyes.
*Source: to be added to site (thesis station + cards) before drafting.*

**4. The grammar of the gift.**
Natan vs lakach across the Torah: Genesis the book of taking, Deuteronomy the book of giving; hinneh natati — the gift arrives by directing the gaze. The sixteen stations, Genesis to Moab.
*Source: thesis tab; arc tab; essay §2–3.*

**5. See, good, take — and the counter-scenes.**
The formula and its three endings (bless / take / give): Eve, the sons of God, Yocheved. Power's grammar (she was seen, she was taken); Shem and Yefet's averted gaze; Abram's refusal; the Akedah's seeing-that-provides; Joseph. ★ EXPAND: Joseph deserves his own full section — the coveted tunic, Potiphar's wife who "lifted her eyes" at him (39:7, the idiom in full), the refusal, and "you intended evil; God intended good" as the redeemed reframe of an entire life.
*Source: thesis/readings cards; Joseph material partly new.*

**6. The law of the eye.**
Sinai legislates perception: the tenth commandment quotes the fall (nechmad + ta'avah); the third lifts the same verb (nasa); law as inverted quotation of narrative; Deut 4:19; the tzitzit verse — heart and eyes, the two scouts.
*Source: readings; leviticus tab (lo tisa card); essay §2.*

**7. The tests.**
The method chapter, told as narrative: lexical fields, pre-registered predictions, Prophets and Writings runs, Achan, Job 1:21, the outcome test, the 79 collisions and their four signals, the permutation nulls — and the miss, reported (eye+desire in the Writings). The rising give:take ratio across the canon (1.5 → 1.7 → 3.9); the two books with no taking (Song of Songs, Ecclesiastes... and what Ecclesiastes does instead).
*Source: tests tab; arc tab; essay §4–5.*

**8. The skeptic's chapter.** ★ NEW
Steelman the objections in the author's own voice: "any theme can be found everywhere" (answer: pre-registration and the reported miss); cherry-picking (answer: the full-scan tool, public); translation-dependence (answer: every claim at T1 in the Hebrew); gematria (answer: T4, poetry, placed last); what would falsify the thesis. This chapter buys the credibility the rest of the book spends.
*Source: new; draws on tests tab caveats and About tab.*

## Part II — The system within
*The same gaze, turned on the self.*

**9. Shame — the eye that learned lack looks in the mirror.**
Before any punishment: fig leaves and hiding. Shame devours (Jer 3:24's own verb); the first garment vs the garment God gives; shame's claim — that being seen consumes you.
*Source: shame tab.*

**10. The critic — the bodyguard who became the warden.**
The second voice ("Who told you that you were naked?" — God knows He never said it); the doubled heart; Job's comforters fired by name; Zechariah 3 — the accuser dismissed, the filthy garments exchanged.
*Source: critic tab.*

**11. The hardened heart.**
Chazak, kaved, stone. Pharaoh's grammar — reflex, habit, fate; the Rambam: his punishment was losing the ability to return; v'lo shillach — hardness defined as refusing release; Nabal and David, the controlled experiment.
*Source: heart tab.*

**12. The hands — the heart at the end of the arm.**
The sent hand (Gen 3:22's feared gesture performed as rescue by Noah, as blessing by Jacob); closed fist / closed heart (Deut 15:7–8); Refidim — heavy hands held until they become emunah; the lifted palms; clean palms and a pure heart. The two trees are one tree; the medicine is the same fruit, differently held.
*Source: heart tab hand cards; readings (one-tree, matan Torah).*

**13. The return protocol.**
Leviticus 26 as law of teshuvah: confess, let the covered heart be humbled, accept consequences — then. Circumcise the heart; the promise that God will; stone to flesh; the broken heart as offering. ★ ADD: shmita and yovel as the return protocol at the scale of land and economy — "the land is Mine" (Lev 25:23): the give-back institutionalized, coveting starved on a calendar.
*Source: heart tab; jubilee material new.*

**14. Hinneh / Hineni — the twenty-first word.**
One suffix apart; the ayeka that got no answer; Abraham's three; the chain of the available; the seneh at 115 — the self in the fire, not devoured; Moses afraid and staying; the Giver's own Hineni (Isa 58:9) — conditioned on the opened hand.
*Source: hineni tab; essay §7.*

## Part III — The architecture
*The thesis, built into the text's body.*

**15. The two tens.**
Ten utterances, ten words; both tenth items about the gaze (hinneh natati / lo tachmod); the twenty-first word — na'aseh v'nishma, and hineni as what lies beyond both decalogues.
*Source: tens tab; essay §3.*

**16. Leviticus — the ring around the veiled center.**
The chiasm as the project's inversion-device at book scale; the takers at the doorway; the veiled gaze; give as the only verb inside; confession; the goat that lifts; kapporet/Kippur — atonement as Eden's garment-remedy installed as furniture.
*Source: leviticus tab.*

**17. The serpent's number.**
The 358 circuit: nachash → choshen (the anagram on the heart) → mashiach and the anointing → korbano, his offering (the number given back) → look-and-live (healing as redirected gaze) → Nechushtan (even the remedy smashed when stared AT instead of THROUGH) → the poison pronounced as truth: vihyitem/Ehyeh, one verb; emet = 21² = Ehyeh squared. Framed explicitly as T4 with T1 anchors.
*Source: serpent tab; gematria tab.*

**18. Written in a heart.**
The scroll's own body: bet…lamed = lev at Torah scale and canon scale; the Writings bound in El; the emet seals; the middles (the serpent's belly and the doubled seeking; the breast and the covering); atbash — Jeremiah's cipher that spells heart; the heart formed only at the turn — teshuvah in the binding; scale-invariance: the same figure at seven scales.
*Source: heart tab scroll cards; gematria tab; essay §"Written in a heart".*

**19. The good eye.** ★ NEW — the practice chapter
The tradition's own name for the healed gaze: ayin tovah vs ayin ra'ah (Pirkei Avot 2:9); the practices as gaze-training already installed: blessings before enjoyment (the berakhah as licensing the gaze — behold before you consume); Shabbat and manna-logic (enough-ness on a schedule); tzitzit; nesiat kapayim; the daily Ashrei ("You open Your hand"). What a reader does on Monday with all of this.
*Source: mostly new; manna, Ps 145, hands material exists.*

**20. What it comes to.**
Behold — behold me — behold Me. The whole Tanakh in three inflections of one word. The closing returns to the author's own story: the same inversion, noticed now in a life.
*Source: essay closing; new personal material.*

## What must be added for coherence (priority order)

1. **The personal thread (critical).** The site is evidence; a book needs a narrator. Open every Part — ideally every chapter — with a short first-person beat: why you were looking, what the finding changed. Without this it's a compendium; with it, it's a book people give each other.
2. **The evidence ladder (critical).** The T1–T4 tier system, declared in the front matter and stamped on every claim. It is the single device that lets corpus statistics, derash, and gematria coexist in one book without the strongest material being dragged down by the most speculative.
3. **Cain (major gap in the argument).** The fall's first consequence-story is a gaze story (sha'ah, the fallen face, se'et, teshukah) and it is absent from the current system. Should be added to the site as cards/station first, then written as chapter 3.
4. **Joseph (major gap).** The Torah's longest narrative, saturated with the system's vocabulary (the tunic, the lifted eyes of 39:7, the goat, the reframe of 50:20) — currently only props, no treatment.
5. **The skeptic's chapter (credibility).** Steelman objections; state what would falsify the thesis.
6. **The good-eye practice chapter (audience).** Ayin tovah (Avot 2:9), berakhot, Shabbat — turns the book from analysis into something usable; this is what earns the second printing.
7. **Shmita/yovel section** in the return chapter — the protocol at economic scale, completing heart → hands → land.
8. **Hebraist review pass** of every claim before any draft goes out (process, not content).
